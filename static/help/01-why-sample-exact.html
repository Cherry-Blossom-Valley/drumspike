1. Why sample-exact matters

A trigger or a sample replacer fires a new sound for every drum hit in a close-mic track. If it fires late, the new sound smears the original. One millisecond of delay is enough: the close mic and the triggered sound filter each other, and the attack loses its punch.

What goes wrong without it

DAW transient detectors work on loudness. They fire on a wrong sample, or they fire twice on one hit, or they fire on another drum that leaks into the mic. Every one of these is audible.

What Drum Spike does

Drum Spike finds every drum hit in a close-mic track. It puts one MIDI note and one spike on the first sample of the attack of the hit. It does this per drum track, so a snare mic yields snare hits and nothing else.
