5. Export

Export writes the hits as MIDI and spike files for your DAW. The files carry the time of the session, so they line up in Pro Tools. After this chapter the main job is done: tracks in, hits found, files out.

Steps

  1. File > Export… (Cmd+E). The Export dialog opens.
  2. Choose per track a MIDI file and a spike WAV, or one multi-track MIDI file for all tracks.
  3. Choose the spike type: - Synthetic: a short click. - Audio: the hit's own audio from the onset over its body. Drum Spike low-passes the track below 800 Hz and follows the first three clear swings of that low tone (a swing counts at 25 % of the largest of the first three). The snippet ends on a zero crossing, at most 60 ms. A kick gives about 15 to 25 ms, a snare about 5 to 10 ms. - Both: a synthetic spike and an audio spike.
  4. Pick the destination folder and export.
  5. File > Export Again (Option+Cmd+E) repeats the last export with the same settings. This is handy after an edit: export again and the DAW gets the new files. The spike files are written in blocks, so an export of a long track does not hold the whole file in memory.

this demo kick: 8.9 ms (the length the Audio Spike rule measures on this hit). Real kicks usually land at 15 to 25 ms, snares at 5 to 10 ms.

The Audio Spike in numbers: Drum Spike copies the hit's own audio from the onset over its body. The low tone below 800 Hz drives the length: the first three clear swings of that tone, cut at the zero crossing, at most 60 ms. A kick lands around 15 to 25 ms, a snare around 5 to 10 ms. The click spike stays available: it is the synthetic option in the same dialog.

The MIDI file carries every hit at its sample. The export picks the ticks per quarter note from the track's sample rate and the tempo: one tick is one sample when the session has one tempo and 60 x rate / BPM is a whole number of 32767 or less; otherwise the export message states the bound that the tick grid leaves, in samples, when it is over half a sample. The spike file is sample exact at every tempo and rate. A DAW that takes its tempo from the file's tempo event instead of its own session tempo can drift by about 1.9 samples in five minutes; set the session tempo and import the notes without the tempo.

More in the Export dialog:

  • Markers in MIDI writes one marker event per review marker into the one multi-track file; Compensate sampler offset moves the MIDI notes of calibrated tracks by their measured sampler offset. The project remembers both checkboxes.
  • The result line has a Show in Finder button, and the Drag MIDI and Drag Spikes tiles drag the exported files straight into your DAW. Dragging MIDI out of a project without a tempo asks the same question first.
  • A MIDI export of a project without a tempo asks first: the notice names the three ways to set the tempo (Track > Detect Tempo, File > Import Tempo Map, the Tempo ruler) and offers Set Tempo…, Export anyway and Cancel.
  • An export of one spike type also rewrites the older spike file of the other type in the same folder, from the same hits.

To export the selected track only, use Track > Export Selected Tracks… (Shift+Cmd+E); it opens the Export dialog with that track.
