5. Export

Export writes the hits as MIDI and spike files for your DAW. The files carry the time of the session, so they line up in Pro Tools. After this chapter the main job is done: tracks in, hits found, files out.

Steps

  1. File > Export… (Cmd+E). The Export dialog opens.
  2. Choose per track a MIDI file and a spike WAV, or one multi-track MIDI file for all tracks.
  3. Choose the spike type: - Synthetic: a short click. - Audio: the hit's own audio from the onset over its body. Drum Spike low-passes the track below 800 Hz and follows the first three clear swings of that low tone (a swing counts at 25 % of the largest of the first three). The snippet ends on a zero crossing, at most 60 ms. A kick gives about 15 to 25 ms, a snare about 5 to 10 ms. - Both: a synthetic spike and an audio spike.
  4. Pick the destination folder and export.
  5. File > Export Again (Option+Cmd+E) repeats the last export with the same settings. This is handy after an edit: export again and the DAW gets the new files. The spike files are written in blocks, so an export of a long track does not hold the whole file in memory.
